**2001: A Kernel Space Odyssey**

*You thought Duke Nukem Forever had a long and troubled birth? Fourteen
years. Amateurs.*

In 2001, three ~~nerds~~ computer scientists with a weakness for
artificial intelligence, operating systems and functional languages had
an idea. They would write a real operating system, as an excuse to try
what nobody else was trying. A microkernel. Services as ordinary
processes. OCaml's virtual machine as a first-class citizen. And old home
computers brought back to life as the most inconvenient serial terminals
imaginable: a ZX Spectrum chatting with MyNOS over a cable, because why
not.

Then real life happened. It stopped the work. It did not stop the
passion.

Twenty-five years later, artificial intelligence lent them the extra
hands they had always lacked, and they took their revenge on real life.
This time, the AI opened the pod bay doors. The kernel of 2002 is still
the kernel. The ideas of 2002, they insist, were good, and fun, and
needed only finishing.

Were they visionaries then, or have they simply lost it since? Posterity
will judge.

What is certain is the result: **MyNOS**, a small microkernel operating
system. Its file systems, network and windows run as ordinary processes
and talk through typed messages, and most of them are written in OCaml.
It even has an AI agent that writes and runs its own programs, inside the
system. Small enough to be read from end to end, and this book shows you
how.
