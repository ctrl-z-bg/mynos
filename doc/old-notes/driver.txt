 There are four methods:
  -syscall based (in and out calls)
  -protection fault based (described above)
  -memory mapped i/o space (you catch the pagefault, do the i/o, fill the
   registers, then return)
  -tss based port checking (fastest, but needs some hacking to be optimal)

 On the x86 architecture, you can only do the ones above. What else you
 need? (there is a software, two hardware assisted, and one fully hardware
 based solution...)

> and at the end, can anybody tell for sure, whats faster, tss switch or
> software switch?

 The software one could be faster. (if well written)

 You only have to:
  -save the old registers (or only a subset)
  -load a new address space (CR3)
  -load the new registers

 The segment registers could be just loaded by mov-s, and in case of a
 per cpu kernel stack system, you don't have to worry about anything else.
 
 When you use multiple kernel stacks, and save registers on kernel entry,
 then you only have to:
  -save old stack pointer (esp0)
  -load a new address space
  -load a new stack pointer
 And you are in another thread's kernel stack.

 Remember, when you switch to another thread of the same process, you don't
 have to reload CR3. (much faster!)
